Top Things to Do in Tbilisi: From Old Town to Georgian Wine

Tbilisi is a city of striking contrasts. Wooden balconies lean over narrow streets, centuries-old churches overlook the Mtkvari River, and modern landmarks sit comfortably alongside the Old Town. Between sightseeing stops, there are sulfur baths to enjoy, Georgian dishes to discover, and excellent local wines to try. If you're looking for the best things to do in Tbilisi, start with its historic neighborhoods and leave time for the views from the surrounding hills. These twelve experiences bring together the city's best-known sights, cultural traditions, and a few places worth exploring at a slower pace.

Updated October 27, 2026

Explore Old Tbilisi's Courtyards and Balconies

Old Tbilisi is a good place to begin. Around Meidan Square, winding lanes lead past carved wooden balconies, small courtyards, and buildings that reflect the city's diverse architectural history. You can spend an enjoyable morning here without following a strict itinerary.

Walk towards Sioni Cathedral, then take a detour through the smaller streets nearby. You'll find cafés, traditional houses, and glimpses of the hillside above the rooftops. It's an area best explored on foot, although cobblestones and steep stretches call for comfortable shoes.

From here, several other attractions are just a short walk away, making the Old Town an easy starting point for your visit. Allow time for the details that don't appear on most sightseeing maps: a carved doorway, a courtyard garden, or a balcony overlooking the street.

Discover the Curious Gabriadze Clock Tower

On Shavteli Street, the unusual clock tower beside the Rezo Gabriadze Marionette Theatre is one of Tbilisi's most recognizable sights. Its crooked silhouette, mismatched details, and painted tiles make it look as though it belongs in a storybook.

A small angel appears to strike the bell every hour, and a miniature puppet show takes place at scheduled times. Check the current timetable if you'd like to see the performance.

Before heading towards the river, walk past nearby Anchiskhati Basilica, one of the city's oldest surviving churches. The clock tower and basilica are close enough to visit together, without planning a separate outing. This part of the historic center rewards visitors who enjoy discovering landmarks just around the corner from one another.

Cross the Bridge of Peace and Explore Rike Park

Few places show Tbilisi's contrasts as clearly as the Bridge of Peace. Its curved glass-and-steel canopy stretches across the Mtkvari River, linking the historic center with Rike Park.

From the bridge, look towards Metekhi Church on the rocky riverbank and the older buildings rising behind it. On the opposite side, Rike Park provides open space for a stroll and a convenient departure point for the cable car.

The bridge is worth seeing again in the evening, when its lights reflect in the water. During the day, the views towards Metekhi and the older neighborhoods offer plenty of opportunities for photographs. For daytime sightseeing, combine this stop with the cable car rather than crossing the river again later.

Take in the Views from Tbilisi's Cable Car

The cable car from Rike Park is one of the easiest ways to see Tbilisi from above. During the short ride, the city opens out below: red rooftops, churches, bridges, and the winding river all come into view.

At the top, enjoy the viewpoints near Narikala Fortress and walk along the ridge towards Kartlis Deda, the Mother of Georgia statue. The figure carries a bowl of wine for friends and a sword for enemies, a familiar symbol of Georgian hospitality and resilience.

The fortress walls remain an unmistakable part of the skyline. The ridge and nearby viewpoints provide the experience without needing to enter the fortress itself. From here, the paths descend towards Abanotubani, although some sections are steep. If you prefer not to walk downhill, return by cable car and make your way to the baths from the historic center.

For a longer journey, the Best of Georgia private tour connects Tbilisi’s historic landmarks with the mountain landscapes of Kazbegi and other destinations.

Relax in the Sulfur Baths of Abanotubani

Tbilisi's sulfur baths are part of the city's history. According to local legend, the area's natural hot springs inspired the founding of the Georgian capital. Today, the brick domes of Abanotubani are among the Old Town's best-known sights.

Look for the blue-tiled façade of Chreli Abano, then choose between public facilities and a private bathing room, depending on the bathhouse. A traditional kisa scrub can be added at some locations.

Private rooms are worth booking in advance, especially on weekends. The facilities and treatments vary between bathhouses, so it's worth choosing one that suits the experience you have in mind. After your visit, follow the path into Leghvtakhevi Gorge, where a waterfall appears unexpectedly between the cliffs. It's an easy addition while you're already in the neighborhood.

Admire Tbilisi's Holy Trinity Cathedral

The Holy Trinity Cathedral, usually called Sameba, stands high above the city on Elia Hill. Completed in 2004, its golden dome is visible from many parts of Tbilisi, making it one of the capital's defining landmarks.

The cathedral's spacious grounds offer a calmer setting than the busy Old Town streets. Inside, visitors can appreciate the scale of the church and its place in Georgian Orthodox life.

Sameba sits across the river from much of the historic center, so it's sensible to plan it as a separate stop rather than repeatedly crossing between neighborhoods. As it's an active place of worship, dress respectfully and observe any visitor guidance at the entrance.

Discover Art and Antiques at Dry Bridge Market

Dry Bridge Market is one of Tbilisi's most enjoyable places to browse. Artists and vendors spread paintings, old books, vintage cameras, jewelry, and Soviet-era objects across stalls and outdoor displays.

You don't have to be shopping for anything in particular. The variety is part of the attraction, and the surrounding streets often feature artwork by local painters. Visit during the day, when the market is liveliest; the number of sellers can vary with the weather.

For a different shopping experience, explore the boutiques on Abashidze Street in Vake. If international labels are more your interest, Tbilisi Outlet Village is an option outside the center. Plan the outlet as a separate trip and check its current stores and transport details on the official website before going. It's an optional addition for those who enjoy shopping, rather than a necessary stop on a first visit to Tbilisi.

Explore Georgian History at the National Museum

For a closer look at Georgia's past, visit the Simon Janashia Museum of Georgia on Rustaveli Avenue. Part of the Georgian National Museum network, it houses archaeological discoveries and collections that illuminate the country's long cultural history.

The exhibits add context to the religious buildings, historic towns, and traditions you'll encounter elsewhere on a journey through Georgia. It's also a useful stop when the weather makes a long outdoor walk less appealing.

The museum pairs naturally with a stroll along Rustaveli Avenue and a later walk towards Freedom Square. This part of the city makes a useful contrast to the narrow lanes of the Old Town, with broad avenues and grand nineteenth-century buildings. Check opening hours and exhibitions before visiting, as museum schedules can change.

Admire the Architecture of Sololaki

Just beyond Freedom Square, Sololaki offers a quieter side of Tbilisi. Its streets are lined with elegant houses from the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, some with ornate entrances, Art Nouveau details, and intricately decorated balconies.

Rather than searching for one famous monument, take time to notice the building details as you walk. Many of the most attractive entrances belong to residential properties, so admire them respectfully without entering private spaces.

Sololaki is also a pleasant place to stop for coffee before continuing towards the lower funicular station. If you enjoy architecture, it's worth giving the neighborhood some time rather than treating it as a quick shortcut between attractions. The change of pace feels welcome after the busier streets around the Old Town.

Watch the Sunset from Mtatsminda

For another perspective on the city, take the historic funicular up Mtatsminda. The hilltop park has restaurants, family attractions, and broad views over Tbilisi and the surrounding landscape.

The Mtatsminda funicular and the Rike Park cable car are two different journeys, leading to separate hills. If you're visiting both, plan them for different parts of your stay rather than traveling back and forth across the center in a single afternoon.

Try Traditional Georgian Food

Georgian food deserves time in any Tbilisi itinerary. Start with khinkali, dumplings filled with meat or other ingredients, and khachapuri, the country's much-loved cheese-filled bread. Both come in different regional styles, so there's always something new to try even if you've tasted them before.

Also look for badrijani nigvzit, eggplant rolls with a walnut filling, and pkhali, vegetable spreads seasoned with herbs and walnuts. They're ideal for sharing alongside other small plates.

A leisurely meal is a good way to experience Georgian hospitality, especially after a day of sightseeing. Travelers interested in going beyond the restaurant menu can join a cooking class to learn how traditional dishes are prepared.

Discover Georgia's Qvevri Wine Traditions

Georgia's winemaking traditions go back thousands of years, and Tbilisi has plenty of places to explore them. In a local wine bar or tasting room, ask about qvevri wines, made using large clay vessels traditionally used for fermentation and storage.

A guided tasting is a good introduction to Georgian grape varieties, including Saperavi and Rkatsiteli, as well as the different methods used to produce them. It also offers insight into the important role wine plays in the country's social traditions.

If wine is a particular interest, consider extending your trip to Kakheti, the renowned wine region east of the capital. You can read more about the country’s wine traditions and other destinations in Firebird Tours’ Georgia Travel Guide.

A Short Video Guide to Tbilisi

Tbilisi is a city best explored without rushing, from its winding Old Town streets to the hilltop views and lively tables shared over Georgian food and wine. If you're planning to stay a little longer, explore Tbilisi tour options to see how the city can fit into a wider journey through Georgia.
